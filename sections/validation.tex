\section{Validation}
\label{sec:validation}

Two predictions are checked by simulation: that the bias bound \eqref{eq:Bdelta}
is tight (the realized bias grows linearly in $\delta$ at the predicted slope), and
that the singleton sample complexity follows the $r/\gamma^2$ rate of
\eqref{eq:samplecomplexity}. Both reuse the per-domain data-generating processes of
the sibling papers, instrumented with the single tilt knob $\delta$ of
\cref{def:tilt}; the shared harness is \texttt{scripts/sensitivity\_sweep.R},
base R with a fixed seed and no external data, run by \texttt{make sim}. In every
case the analytic line is the theorem's own formula, the slope
$\Info^{-1}\Cov(s, h)$ computed from the model's score and tilt rather than fitted,
so a match is a genuine confirmation and not a curve fit.

\paragraph{Sensitivity sweep.} For each domain we draw data under
$\mathrm{C2}(\delta)$ for a grid of $\delta$, fit the face-value MLE, and record the
realized parameter bias against the first-order prediction $\delta\,
\Info^{-1}\Cov(s, h)$ of \eqref{eq:bias}. The prediction is confirmed if the
realized bias is linear in $\delta$ through the origin with slope matching the
analytic constant; this slope is the worst-case edge of the zonotope
$\mathcal{S}_\delta$ of \cref{cor:partial} along the tested tilt, so the scalar check
verifies the bound $\delta B(\theta^\star)$ that bounds that set without simulating
its full geometry. The exponential-family domains (single-cell, spatial,
phenotyping, the discrete weak-supervision parametrization) should track the
prediction with no $O(\delta^2)$ curvature along the curved directions; the
location-family domain (differential privacy) should track it up to the
$O_p(n^{-1/2})$ remainder.

\paragraph{Singleton sample complexity.} For a domain with a tunable rank deficit
$r$ and margin $\gamma$ we add $n_{\mathrm{s}}$ singletons and record the restored
parameter's squared error as a function of $n_{\mathrm{s}}$, $r$, and $\gamma$. The
prediction \eqref{eq:samplecomplexity} is confirmed if the error scales as
$1/n_{\mathrm{s}}$, the singletons needed for a fixed accuracy scale linearly in
$r$, and the dependence on the margin is $\gamma^{-2}$. The weak-supervision
$r$-sweep of \citet{towell2026weaksupcoarsening} is one already-reproduced slice of
this surface.

\paragraph{Results: the bias bound is tight.} We instantiate two representative
regimes (\cref{fig:sweep}). The exponential-family regime is a Bernoulli latent
with value-dependent coarsening: each unit is either reported exactly (an
informative singleton) or coarsened to an uninformative full candidate set, with
the keep-weight tilted by the true value, $\kappa(\text{keep}\mid y) =
\kappa_0\exp\{\delta(y-p^\star)\}$, the textbook value-dependent-missingness form
of \cref{def:tilt}. Concretely this is \cref{def:tilt} with the coarsen report's
candidate set $\{0, 1\}$ and within-set tilt $h(r, y) = y - p^\star$, whose
variation across that set (slope $\delta$) is the C2 violation. The location-family
regime is the differential-privacy
mechanism $M = \mu + Z$ with a data-dependent Gaussian tilt of scale set by
$\sigma^2/c$. The predictions hold exactly where the theory says they should.
On the natural scale the exponential-family bias is exactly $\delta$ at every
$\delta$ (the report law stays an exponential family with a $\delta$-shifted
natural parameter), and the simulation recovers a slope of $0.9999$ with $R^2 =
1.000$ against the predicted slope of $1$, confirming the exactness-along-curved-directions
claim of \cref{thm:sensitivity}; on the mean scale the realized bias tracks the
closed-form pseudo-true curve to within $1.2\%$ over the whole grid, with the
straight first-order line $\delta\,\Info^{-1}\Cov(s,h)$ of slope $0.227$ peeling
off only at the expected $O(\delta^2)$ rate. The location-family domain matches its
predicted slope of $0.667$ with a realized slope of $0.667$ ($R^2 = 1.000$, ratio
$1.000$), the residual being the anticipated $O_p(n^{-1/2})$ Monte Carlo term.
Both bias directions are exactly those the marginal-fit check cannot see, as the
remark after \cref{thm:sensitivity} predicts.

\paragraph{Results: the $r/\gamma^2$ rate holds.} The restoration domain has a
tunable rank deficit $r$ and margin $\gamma$, with singletons supplying direct
noisy observations along the confounded subspace $V$ (\cref{fig:complexity}).
Fixing $r$ and $\gamma$, the restored squared error decays as
$n_{\mathrm{s}}^{-1.01}$, the $1/n_{\mathrm{s}}$ law. Fixing $r$ and
$n_{\mathrm{s}}$, the error scales as $\gamma^{-2.01}$, the inverse-square margin
dependence. For the singleton budget at fixed accuracy the simulation makes the
fork in the \cref{thm:restoration} sketch explicit: at a fixed \emph{per-direction}
target, the budget grows as $r^{1.02}$, the linear-in-$r$ rate of
\eqref{eq:samplecomplexity}; at a fixed \emph{total} squared-error target it grows
as $r^{1.98}$, the quadratic balanced-floor-split rate. The $r/\gamma^2$ statement
of \cref{thm:restoration} is therefore the per-confounded-direction reading, the
one the program's singleton-budget interpretation intends: each of the $r$
confounded directions needs $\Theta(1/\gamma^2)$ singletons restored to a fixed
accuracy, so $\Theta(r/\gamma^2)$ in all. All three exponents agree with the
predictions to within Monte Carlo error.
